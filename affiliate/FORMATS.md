# Formats — why your IG carousels get <100 views, and what to post instead

## The diagnosis

Warming the account helps, but it is not why your Instagram carousels die.

**Instagram carousels are shown mostly to people who already follow you.**
Carousel reach rate is ~14% of an audience versus ~31% for Reels — and 14% of
zero followers is zero views. Your five brand accounts are posting the one format
that has no cold-start engine on that platform. That is the whole mechanism.

On TikTok the opposite is true: photo carousels get pushed to strangers from post
one, which is exactly why your bands there are 100–800 instead of <100.

Two consequences:

1. **Instagram needs video to start.** Reels and Explore are how a 0-follower
   account gets seen. The carousel is what converts the people the Reel brought.
2. **Trial Reels are not available yet.** The feature that shows a Reel *only* to
   non-followers requires **1,000 followers**. Put it on the D30 list; it's a
   strong lever once you qualify, not a launch tactic.

## Your warm-up theory: keep it, but change its shape

Liking and following inside the niche does seed the topical cluster the account
gets recommended into, and it costs nothing. Two corrections:

- **It's a daily habit, not a phase.** 15 minutes a day per account, forever:
  watch niche carousels to the last slide, save a few, and — most valuable —
  leave early, non-generic comments on big accounts in the niche. On Instagram
  that's also the cheapest source of profile visits you have in week one.
- **It cannot fix a format problem.** No amount of warming makes a carousel
  reach non-followers on IG. Warm *and* post Reels.

## The format ladder — per account, per day

| Account | Carousels | Video | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok A | 3–5 | **2 template videos** | video pulls ~5× the plays of a photo post; the carousels do the saving and sending |
| Instagram A | 1 | **2 Reels** | Reels are the only cold-reach surface until 1k |
| TikTok B | 3–5 | **1–2** | text-on-screen video over a trending sound |
| Instagram B | 1 | **1–2 Reels** | one of them the screen recording of a real automation |

The videos are not extra work if they are built from slides you already
rendered: the same 6 JPGs at 0.8s each with a trending audio *is* a Reel.

## CapCut templates — the fastest reach per minute of work

Using a **trending template** inherits the trend's audio and its edit rhythm,
which is the part the algorithm is already distributing. This is the single best
idea in this document and it costs ~10 minutes a video.

How to pick one that's actually rising:
1. On TikTok, scroll your niche and look for the **"CapCut · try this template"**
   link under videos that are doing well.
2. Open it and check the use count. **Under ~100k uses and rising** is the
   window. Over a million and you're late.
3. Prefer templates that hold a still image for 1–2 seconds — those are the ones
   built for product photos or text slides, not for face cuts.

Batch it: one 45-minute sitting on the phone produces 5–8 videos, which is two
days of both accounts' video slots. Export without the CapCut watermark
(finish → save, then post natively; never post a video with another app's
watermark, both platforms demote it).

Lane A videos: the annoyance in the first frame, the product in frame 2, the
result in frame 3. Lane B videos: three lines of text over a screen recording.

## The actress / AI-model idea — no, and here's the arithmetic

It would get views. It would not get money, and it would cost you the ceiling:

- **The audience doesn't convert.** That traffic buys nothing in either niche.
  The only offers that monetize it are dating, AI-companion and betting programs
  — the sketchiest payout tier there is, with the highest chargeback and
  program-death rate.
- **Real actresses' photos are someone else's rights.** Copyright plus right of
  publicity. That's a takedown-and-ban path with no appeal, after you've spent
  months building the account.
- **AI-generated people must be labeled as AI on TikTok, and labeled content is
  reach-suppressed.** You'd pay a reach tax on the one strategy whose entire
  argument was reach.
- **It forecloses the money.** No brand pays that account for UGC, no US
  business hires it to build automations, and the $300–1,500/month retainers —
  the highest line in `ACCOUNTS.md` — are off the table permanently.

If what you actually want is the *visual pull* of that content with an audience
that buys, the legitimate version is **cheap aesthetic finds**: outfits, beauty,
room aesthetics. Temu's Asset Center is strongest in exactly those categories,
outfit carousels are the native photo-mode format on TikTok, and the audience
buys $15 things on impulse. It's saturated and the commissions stay small, but
it's real.

**Decision rule:** keep lane A on home fixes — higher ticket, your own photos,
and it keeps the UGC/brand-deal door open. Add one "room aesthetic" post a week
as a probe. If lane A's band is still dead at D14, swap the whole account to
aesthetic finds rather than opening a new one.
