# The four accounts — one job each

Two lanes × two platforms is **not** four copies of the same play. TikTok and
Instagram reward different things, so each account gets one job, one primary
metric, and one thing it is forbidden to do.

The asymmetry that drives all of it:

| | TikTok | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| Cold reach | Enormous, from post #1, to strangers | Weak for carousels (~14% reach rate); **Reels are the discovery surface** (~31%) |
| Link | **None until 1,000 followers** | Bio link day one **+ comment-to-DM** |
| Strongest signal | saves, then shares, per-slide dwell | **sends** (DM shares), then watch time, then likes |
| Format that wins | photo carousels + trending sound | Reels for reach, carousels for depth |
| What it's for here | **finding people** | **converting them** |

TikTok makes the audience. Instagram makes the money — **but not exclusively,
and that was an error in the first version of this file.**

Two corrections, both from evidence:

1. **TikTok can sell from day zero without the bio link.** DM privacy set to
   *Everyone* (available at 18+) means "DM me the word FIX and I'll send it"
   works with **0 followers**. Requests land in a folder you clear daily. That
   completely routes around the 1,000-follower link gate, which was the single
   biggest constraint in this plan.
2. **TikTok gets the aggressive cadence, Instagram gets the careful one.** Your
   five Spanish accounts confirm TikTok is where the distribution is; IG
   carousels reach ~14% of an audience. So TikTok goes to 5–6 posts/day after
   the warm-up, and Instagram stays at 1 carousel + 1–2 Reels.

Read [`LESSONS-FROM-YOUR-DATA.md`](LESSONS-FROM-YOUR-DATA.md) before writing a
single post — your own 31–61-post history says exactly which frames break the
band and which CTA produced zero results in 12 tries.

## Why the pairs share a handle, and where they diverge

Re-examined 2026-08-17, because once TikTok can collect leads by DM at zero
followers, it no longer *needs* Instagram — which reopened the question of
whether all four should be independent niches.

**Verdict: keep two niches across four accounts, but run four different plays.**
Four independent niches would mean four content systems fed by one person, and
the evidence says the binding constraint on income is the audience's buying
power and the offer — not the number of niches. Two well-fed pairs beat four
starved accounts. The handle stays shared for exactly two reasons: the TikTok →
IG handoff needs a name people can retype, and search recall compounds across
platforms.

Where they must **not** be the same, and this is new:

| | TikTok | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| Who's there | 18–24 (39%) and 25–34 (40%) — younger, renters, first apartments | 25–34 largest; **56% of US users have household income over $75k**, revenue per US user $249 vs TikTok's $131 |
| So lane A pushes | the **cheap** viral stuff — $8–20, dorm, rental, roommate framing | the **higher-ticket** items — $40–200, homeowner and family framing. Same niche, different price tier, because this audience actually buys |
| So lane B pushes | discovery and authority — ~2/3 of decision-makers on TikTok say they use it to find business software, and 54% of business owners post there, so it is *not* a dead B2B channel | the close — the owners with budget are here, and the comment→DM is where a $1,200 build gets agreed |
| Format | photo carousels + template videos | Reels for reach, carousels for depth |

That is the real answer to "should they be brothers": same name, same niche,
**different price point, different framing, different CTA.** Lane A's Instagram
selling $150 items while its TikTok sells $12 ones is worth more than any
fifth account.

### The idea this investigation killed

Cold DM outreach to local businesses from Instagram B looked like the fastest
path to a first client. It isn't — Meta's rule is **user-initiated only**, new
accounts are capped around 20–50 DMs/day, the caps are now a dynamic behavioural
trust score rather than a fixed number, and cold outreach is the single most
common cause of restriction and shadowban. One ban erases months.

So: **never send a cold DM from these accounts.** The compliant path is the one
already in the plan — comment → auto-DM → conversation, which is user-initiated
by definition, plus replying to people who write first.

## What each account sells

The rule: **TikTok sells the free thing, Instagram sells the paid thing.** A
TikTok viewer is colder and can't click anything for the first weeks, so asking
them to buy burns the impression; asking them to follow or to grab a free list
converts 20× better. Instagram is where a warm person is one DM away from a
link.

| Account | Sells **today** | Sells **at scale** | Month 1 | Month 3 | Month 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TikTok A** | the follow · `DM me FIX` → the list, from 0 followers | bio link at 1k · brand placements $50–300/post | $0 | $0–150 | $100–600 |
| **Instagram A** | Temu + **higher-ticket** links via the `FIX` DM · the email list | Amazon (D30+) · **UGC deals, $150–500/video** | $0–25 | $50–250 | $150–800 |
| **TikTok B** | the free stack list · `DM me STACK` → and the DM thread becomes a lead | bio link at 1k · the top of the service funnel | $0 | $0–100 | $100–500 |
| **Instagram B** | Systeme.io (60% lifetime) · Hostinger ($40–80) · Fiverr (25% of first order) | your own $27–47 pack, 100% margin | $0–60 | $150–700 | $400–2,000 |
| **↳ service + UGC layer** | *(fed by all four — see below)* | one build or retainer a month, plus UGC videos | $0–300 | $700–3,000 | $2,000–8,000 |

**Most likely single outcome** — the mode, not the average, because the
distribution is fat-tailed and the low end is the common case:

| | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 | Month 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliate only | $0 | ~$60 | ~$200 | ~$600 |
| With the service + UGC layer worked properly | $0 | ~$300 | ~$900 | ~$2,500 |

Assumes the cadence holds, no account gets banned, and you answer DMs. Roughly a
**1 in 3 chance of near-zero at month 3 on the affiliate-only path** — that risk
is exactly what the service layer removes, because one client beats a month of
commissions.

Read the two Instagram rows again: **that is where all four accounts' revenue
comes from.** The TikToks are unpaid until they're big, on purpose.

### The ceiling: affiliate is the floor, not the goal

Being blunt about the arithmetic. A month of good posting on lane B might be
100k views → ~400 clicks → 4 conversions → ~$200. **One** US small business
buying a $1,200 automation build — from the same audience, the same content, the
same DM thread — pays six times that, and you already do this work for Peruvian
clients through Qolca at a third of US rates.

So lane B's Instagram sells **two things at two altitudes**:

1. *Passive floor* — the affiliate links, which pay whether or not anyone talks
   to you.
2. *The real money* — "I build this for people. Want me to build yours?" as the
   reply in DMs when someone describes their problem. One client a month is
   the entire target; you do not need an audience of 50,000 to find one.

The same shape applies to lane A: its high-ticket line is **UGC** — brands
paying $150–500 per video for content you already know how to produce. On both
accounts, the affiliate commissions are what pay while the pipeline fills.

**The tax situation actually favors this.** Services you perform from Peru for a
US client are **foreign-source income**: no US withholding, no 1099, nothing to
file — unlike Amazon's affiliate royalties, which are US-source and get docked
30% because Peru has no treaty. The highest-paying line here is also the one with
the least paperwork. Invoice in USD, get paid to PayPal or Wise, sign a W-8BEN if
a client's finance team asks for one.

### Three income levers, ranked by what they actually pay

1. **Done-for-you content, $300–1,500/month retainers.** You produce six
   finished carousels a day from JSON. US small businesses and creators pay for
   exactly that, at US prices — it is Propaga's service sold in English by the
   person who built the machine. Highest ceiling of anything in this folder, and
   the audience of lane B is made of the buyers.
2. **Automation builds, $800–2,000 one-off.** Same DM thread, different ask.
   One client beats a month of commissions.
3. **Raise lane A's ticket.** A $12 Temu gadget pays ~$1. A $150 problem-solver
   pays $5–8. Keep the cheap items — they are what goes viral — but make every
   list post carry one "worth the $150" item, and load the bio page with them.
   Same views, 5–8× the commission.

Below these, in order: UGC videos ($150–500), your own $27 pack, then the
affiliate links that pay while the pipeline fills.

Do not launch with any of them. Post for 30 days, let the audience prove itself,
then add the ask to the DM script. If you add it on day one the account reads as
an agency pitch and the reach never comes.

### The unobvious one: UGC on Instagram A

Once lane A has a few hundred followers and a feed of clean product shots,
small home-goods brands start sliding into DMs asking for content. Making videos
*for* a brand pays $150–500 per deliverable, lands in PayPal in days, needs zero
followers of your own to be worth doing — and you already own a video pipeline
most UGC creators don't. Realistically this out-earns Temu commissions by 10× on
that account. Don't chase it before D30; do answer every brand DM after.

### What each account must never try to sell

- **TikTok A:** anything with a price in the first 5 seconds. It reads as an ad
  and the post dies in the first batch of impressions.
- **Instagram A:** a SaaS subscription. Wrong audience — they came for a $9 drain
  catcher.
- **TikTok B:** a paid tool to a cold viewer. It has no reason to trust you yet;
  sell the free list and let the emails do the selling.
- **Instagram B:** two offers in one post, and anything you don't personally run.

## 1 · TikTok A — `@<handleA>` · the crowd

**Job:** cold reach and followers. Get to 1,000 as fast as possible — that's when
the bio link unlocks and this account stops being a billboard for Instagram.

**Sells:** the follow, plus the list through `DM me FIX` — which works at zero
followers, so this account is no longer unpaid while it grows.

- **Post:** 3/day for the first two weeks, then **5–6 photo carousels/day**.
  Volume's job is buying lottery tickets and finding the frame faster, not reach
  by itself.
- **Every post ends with a send ask naming a person** — "send this to whoever
  does the dishes". That mechanic is what produced your only 27.9K post.
- **DM privacy → Everyone.** Clear the request folder once a day.
- **Sound:** a trending sound with under ~50k uses, volume low. Never silent.
- **Search:** TikTok is a search engine for this niche. Put the literal query on
  slide 1 as on-image text *and* in the caption — "how to stop hair clogging the
  shower drain", not "you NEED this".
- **Playlists:** group posts into `Kitchen fixes`, `Car fixes`, `Renter fixes`.
  Playlists turn one viral post into six views per visitor.
- **Pinned posts:** pin your 3 best. New visitors see those before anything else.
- **CTA:** pinned first comment → `full list → IG @handleA`. Nothing else.
- **Primary metric:** followers per 1,000 views (target: 5+). Views are vanity.
- **Never:** post the same carousel that ran on IG that day, or push the Temu
  link in-feed — TikTok suppresses off-platform commerce pushed too hard, and
  the link doesn't work yet anyway.

## 2 · Instagram A — `@<handleA>` · the register

**Job:** convert lane A's traffic into clicks and emails, today, without waiting
for any follower threshold.

**Sells:** Temu product links (≈$1–4 an order) through the `FIX` auto-DM, and the
email list behind them. From D30: Amazon links alongside Temu, and paid UGC work
for brands that DM you — which will out-earn the commissions.

- **Post:** 1 carousel/day (the same one, IG crop — for saves and sends) **+ 1–2
  Reels/day** built from the same product photos: 6–8 stills, 0.8s each,
  trending audio, one line of on-screen text. Reels are what reach strangers;
  the carousel is what makes them trust you.
- **The mechanic — comment-to-DM:** every carousel and Reel ends with
  `comment FIX and I'll send you the link`. A free automation tool (ManyChat's
  free tier, or InstantDM/LinkDM) sends the link to their inbox automatically.
  Requires the Creator account connected to a Facebook Page — set that up on D2.
  *Use* ManyChat if it's the easiest; never *promote* it, it competes with
  Propaga.
- **Why this beats the bio link:** a comment is a public engagement signal that
  boosts the post, and the DM is a 1-to-1 surface with no third-party hop. Two
  wins from one CTA.
- **Stories:** 2–3/day, link sticker on every one. Reshare the day's post,
  answer a comment, show the product on your desk. Stories are where the small
  follower base actually converts.
- **Primary metric:** DM triggers per post, then link clicks. Followers here
  matter less than on TikTok.
- **Never:** post without a caption disclosure, and never let a Reel be a silent
  slideshow — audio is 40% of the reach.

## 3 · TikTok B — `@<handleB>` · the search desk

**Job:** get in front of US solo operators who are *typing a problem into the
search bar*. This audience doesn't browse the way lane A's does — they search.

**Sells:** the free stack list — and, through the DM thread that follows it, the
service. Never a paid tool to a cold viewer: they have no reason to trust a
recommendation yet.

- **Post:** 3/day for two weeks, then **5–6 text-plate carousels/day**.
- **`DM me STACK` on every post**, DM privacy → Everyone. Every person who
  writes is a qualified lead: they told you their problem to get the list. From
  D30 that thread is where the $800–2,000 build gets sold.
- **Target queries, not topics:** each post answers one literal search — "how to
  follow up with leads automatically", "free tools for a small business",
  "how to make an invoice reminder". That query is the cover line and the first
  five words of the caption.
- **The reach outlier:** the *correction* post ("everyone automates the wrong
  half of this"). Politely disagreeing with the consensus gets shared by people
  who wanted someone to say it. Run one every third day.
- **Sound:** low-key trending audio. This niche tolerates quiet, but silent
  still under-delivers.
- **CTA:** pinned comment → `free stack list → IG @handleB`.
- **Primary metric:** saves per view (target 2%+) and profile visits. This
  account's followers are worth 5–10× lane A's, so a slower count is fine.
- **Never:** money-claim hooks ("how I made $4k"), fake screenshots, or the
  "10 AI tools you NEED" shape. Those get views from people who never buy and
  they poison the recommendation cluster.

## 4 · Instagram B — `@<handleB>` · the money desk

**Job:** the email list and the actual commissions. This is the highest-value of
the four accounts and the one that survives if the other three get throttled.

**Sells:** Systeme.io (60% lifetime recurring — the only line here that
compounds), Hostinger ($40–80 a sale), Fiverr (25% of a first order, closed in
DMs when someone says "I need X done"). Later: your own $27–47 automation pack,
100% margin.

- **Post:** 1 carousel/day + 1 Reel/day. Reels here are a 30–45s screen
  recording of the real automation running — the single most credible thing you
  can publish in this niche, and something no slop account can fake.
- **The mechanic — comment-to-DM:** `comment STACK and I'll send it` → the DM
  delivers the lead magnet ("12 tools and the 6 automations I run") → the
  Systeme.io page captures the email.
- **DMs are the sales floor.** When someone replies, ask what they're trying to
  automate and answer properly. This audience buys $30–90 tools after one real
  conversation; lane A's audience never will. Budget 15 min/day for this.
- **Weekly email:** one problem, one setup, one link. The list works whether or
  not either algorithm likes you this week.
- **Primary metric:** email opt-ins/week, then paid conversions. Not followers,
  not views.
- **Never:** two offers in one post, or an offer you haven't used yourself.
