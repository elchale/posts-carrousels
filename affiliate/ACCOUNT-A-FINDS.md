# Account A — cheap fixes for annoying stuff

> handle: `@________` (fill on D0) · IG: `@________` · engine: **Temu affiliate**
> · secondary (D30+): Amazon Associates

The reach account. Physical products, photo-led, cents per click, and the only
one of the two with a realistic path to a million-view post.

## The angle, and why it isn't "amazon finds"

Every generic finds account opens with the product. This one opens with the
**annoyance**, and the product doesn't appear until slide 3. A stranger scrolling
has no relationship with a $12 gadget; they have a very old relationship with the
hair clogging their shower drain.

> **Post = one household annoyance → the cheap thing that ends it.**

That framing does three things: it survives a cold audience (nobody needs to know
the brand), it makes the save obvious ("I'll need this"), and it gives every post
a hook that is about the reader, not about a listing.

## Voice

Flat, specific, mildly amused. The register of a friend who fixed the thing last
month, not of a store.

- Never "you NEED this" or "game changer" or "obsessed".
- Dollar amounts always rounded and hedged: `about $9`, `under $15`. Prices move;
  a wrong exact price is an FTC problem and a comment-section problem.
- One claim per slide, and only claims you'd defend. No invented statistics, no
  "studies show", no "TikTok made me buy it" when TikTok didn't
  (`cero falacias` carries over from the brand rules).
- Emoji only on slides carrying an emotion, never on product slides.

## Slide template — 7 slides

| # | Role | What's on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **cover** — text plate | The annoyance, in the reader's own words. No product, no price, no brand. |
| 2 | **agitate** — text plate | Why the usual workaround fails ("you clean it, it's back in a week"). |
| 3 | **product** — photo | The thing, in use. Rough price bottom-right. |
| 4 | **product** — photo | Second angle or the result (the clean drain, the closed drawer). |
| 5 | **detail** — photo or plate | The one honest caveat or the sizing note. Caveats are what get saved. |
| 6 | **more** — plate | 3 more fixes in the same room, one line each. Feeds the multi-item saves. |
| 7 | **cta** — plate | `full list → IG @handle` + the disclosure line. |

Multi-product list posts (the `5 things that fixed my kitchen` shape) use the
same skeleton with slides 3–6 as one product each. Run **70% list posts / 30%
single-fix posts** — lists get the reach, single fixes get the click.

## Images — the rule that keeps the account alive

Allowed:
1. **Temu Asset Center creatives.** Dashboard → Asset Center. These exist to be
   posted. This is the default source.
2. **Your own photos.** Anything you own or bought. Best-converting slides by a
   wide margin — a hand in frame beats a white-background render every time.
3. **Your own text plates** from `tools/render.py`.

Forbidden, no exceptions:
- Amazon listing photos (their agreement forbids re-uploading them).
- Any seller's photos scraped off a listing page, Temu included, if they're not
  in the Asset Center.
- AI-generated images of a real product. It misrepresents the item, and TikTok
  suppresses labeled AI content on top of that.

**Do this while you're still in Raleigh:** buy 6–8 items off the shortlist
(~$80–120 total), and shoot them in one 2-hour session — each item on a clean
surface, in-use, and the "after". That is 60–80 original photos, enough for
6–8 weeks of slide 3/4, and it is the single thing that will separate this
account from every dropship page in the niche.

## Hook formulas — 12, use each once before repeating

The cover slide, verbatim shapes:

1. `Nobody warns you about the [thing] until you're already living with it.`
2. `Everyone in this house does [workaround]. It's been solved for $9 the whole time.`
3. `Two kinds of people: the ones who [annoyance] every week, and the ones who bought this once.`
4. `I did this wrong for four years.`
5. `The $10 version works better than the $80 one. Here's what's different.`
6. `Renting? These are the fixes that don't touch a wall.`
7. `Five things in my kitchen that ended a daily annoyance.`
8. `The reason your [drawer / sink / car] looks like that isn't you.`
9. `Things I bought that I forgot about — because they just work.`
10. `If you have to clean it every week, you bought the wrong one.`
11. `Under $15 each. All seven fixed something I complained about out loud.`
12. `My roommate mocked this one. Then bought two.`

Filter every hook through the `copy-carruseles` skill before it ships: no
paragraphs, no repeating a point twice, no smell of sale before the product
slide, and it has to work for someone who has never heard of the account.

## Caption template

```
[hook line, different wording from the cover]

#ad · I earn commission from links · full list on IG @handle

Which one of these do you already own?

#homefinds #kitchengadgets #organizationhack #[room] #cheapfinds
```

Disclosure in the first 100 characters, always — it must be readable before the
"…more" cut. The question at the end is not decoration; comment replies are a
distribution signal and they cost nothing.

## What good looks like

| Metric | Working | Broken |
|---|---|---|
| Saves / views | above 1.5% | below 0.5% → the hook sells instead of naming the problem |
| Shares / views | above 0.8% | below 0.3% → nobody recognizes the annoyance |
| Profile visits / views | above 1.5% | below 0.7% → the CTA slide is invisible |
| Followers / 1k views | above 5 | below 2 → the account has no reason to exist between posts |

Track saves and shares. Views are for morale.
