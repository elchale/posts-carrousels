# CAROUSELS — research + strategy

Research date: **2026-07-28**. Scope: what actually makes a carousel spread on
IG / TikTok / FB (+ LinkedIn for B2B), how they're designed, and a concrete
per-brand strategy for **Propaga**, **ComeHomeTag** and **Qolca**.

Companion to `CONTENT-PLAN\Plan-Contenido-15-Dias-3-Marcas` (the 15-day plan
that already committed to "2 videos per brand, everything else carousels").

> **2026-07-28 round 2 → see `PLAN-180.md`**: the full 60-per-brand production
> plan, the cinematic-plate system (celebrity likeness verdict: real celebs NO
> — legal/platform risk; faceless archetype scenes + dark overlay YES), share
> psychology, and the cross-posting spec. Two amendments to this doc: IG
> carousels now also accept **3:4 (1080×1440)** but still not 9:16; and §5's
> "no people" rule has a controlled exception (cinematic plates, faces never
> visible, 55–75% overlay baked in).

## 0. Confidence labels used in this doc

The carousel-advice space is ~90% SEO blogspam that recycles each other's
numbers, often contradicting the primary studies. Every claim below is tagged:

- **[MEASURED]** — from a study with a stated sample size and methodology.
- **[SOURCED]** — a direct, attributable statement from a platform (e.g. Mosseri).
- **[CRAFT]** — design/copy convention. No hard data, but consistent across
  every practitioner source and it matches how the formats physically work.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** — vendor-blog claim I could not trace to a primary source.
  Listed only where it's load-bearing, so we know not to lean on it.

## 1. The core finding: carousels are not a reach play

This is the single most important thing and almost every blog gets it backwards.

**[MEASURED]** Buffer, 52M+ posts (medians, engagement measured per impression):

| Instagram | Engagement rate | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| Carousels | **6.90%** | baseline |
| Single image | 4.44% | Reels get **2.25×** more |
| Reels | 3.31% | Reels get **1.36×** more (+36%) than carousels |

**[MEASURED]** Socialinsider, 15M IG posts / 417K pages (Oct 2025–Mar 2026) —
different denominator (per follower), same conclusion: carousels 0.50% vs Reels
0.48% in Q2 2026, carousels leading in most quarters. Per-post medians: Reels
~33 comments / 5 shares, carousels ~25 comments / 3 shares — but carousels win
**saves** (37 vs 35).

**[MEASURED]** Buffer, accounts **under 1,000 followers** — the bracket all
three brands are in: Reels **134** median views, carousels **56**, single
images **23**. Carousels edge Reels on likes (7 vs 6).

**[MEASURED]** TikTok, Buffer: video **3.39%** vs photo/carousel **1.92%** —
video wins by 77%. Multiple vendor blogs claim the opposite ("carousels get 81%
more engagement", "photo mode gets 5× more reach") — **[UNVERIFIED]**, traced to
no primary source, and directly contradicted by the largest dataset available.
Do not plan around them.

**[MEASURED]** LinkedIn, Buffer: carousels (PDF document posts) **21.77%**
median ER vs video 7.35%, images 6.52%, text 3.18%. **The highest-engagement
format measured on any platform.** Below-average carousels still beat typical
video posts.

**[MEASURED]** Facebook, Buffer: images 5.20%, video 4.84%, text 4.76%, links
4.43% — under one percentage point separates every format. Format choice matters
less on FB than anywhere else; organic page reach is the binding constraint, not
format.

### What this means for us

> **Reels are how strangers find us. Carousels are how strangers decide about
> us.** A carousel reaches fewer people and converts far more of them.

So the job of a carousel is never "go viral." It's:
1. **Saves** — the strongest lasting-interest signal, and carousels' natural win.
2. **Sends/DM shares** — **[SOURCED]** Mosseri names *sends per reach* as one of
   the three signals that matter most, and the one that reaches new audiences.
   Practically: content someone forwards into a WhatsApp family group.
3. **Profile visits → link click → WhatsApp conversation.**

This matches the 15-day plan's own metric definition (saves, profile visits, DMs
— not views). Carousels are the right call; just don't expect them to be the
discovery engine. That stays with Reels.

## 2. The mechanic that makes carousels structurally different

**[SOURCED]** Adam Mosseri: *"If someone sees your carousel post but they don't
swipe, we'll often give that carousel a second chance and automatically move to
that second piece of media for the viewer."*

No other format gets a second first impression. Two hard consequences:

- **Slide 2 must work as a cover on its own.** Not a continuation ("...and
  here's why"), not slide 1's punchline. It needs its own hook. This is the
  design rule almost nobody follows and it's free reach.
- **Every additional swipe is an engagement event.** The format manufactures
  interaction: a Reel gets one watch-time signal, a 9-slide carousel can emit 8
  swipe signals plus dwell time per slide.

**[CRAFT]** Corollary: the last slide should loop back or CTA, and re-showing
the post to non-swipers means the *design system* has to be recognizable from
any slide, not just the cover.

## 3. Anatomy of a carousel that performs

### Structure **[CRAFT]** — consistent across every practitioner source

```
Slide 1   HOOK        earns the swipe. ~80% of the outcome.
Slide 2   RE-HOOK     stands alone (second-chance rule) + sets the stakes.
Slides 3-8 VALUE      one idea per slide. Never two.
Slide 9   PROOF       stat / testimonial / screenshot / before-after.
Slide 10  CTA         one action, spelled out. "Guarda esto", not "link in bio".
```

### Slide count

- **[MEASURED-ish]** IG allows 20; 8–10 is the consistently cited sweet spot,
  6–10 for typical posts. Under 5 reads as a short post; past ~10 swipe fatigue.
- **[CRAFT]** TikTok photo mode: **5–10**. Past 8 hurts completion rate, and
  completion rate is what TikTok ranks on.
- **[CRAFT]** LinkedIn: 5–15 pages.
- **Our default: 8 slides.** Works everywhere without a re-cut.

### The hook slide

**[CRAFT]** Formula that every source converges on:

```
BOLD HEADLINE (5–8 words, biggest text on the slide)
+ VISUAL PATTERN INTERRUPT (high contrast / a face / a dramatic mark)
+ CURIOSITY GAP (a partial reveal, a number, or a question you can't answer
  without swiping)
```

Six psychological triggers to rotate through: **curiosity, loss, promise, proof,
contradiction, question.** Users scroll 3–4 posts per second — the hook has a
fraction of a second.

Healthy benchmarks **[CRAFT]**: slide-1→2 swipe-through **60–75%** (under 50% =
the hook failed). Save rate **1.5–3%** of impressions for educational carousels
(under 0.5% = the value slides aren't earning their slot). Both are visible in
IG Insights, so these are testable, not vibes.

### Content archetypes that get saved

Ranked by fit for our brands:

1. **Checklist / "what to do in the first X minutes"** — highest save rate;
   people save it *because they might need it later*. Perfect for ComeHomeTag.
2. **Listicle (5/7/10 things)** — scannable, one item per slide, trivially
   templated.
3. **Myth-buster** — bold wrong belief on the cover, correction inside. Best
   hook-to-effort ratio and doubles as objection handling.
4. **Before/after** — "before" as the cover, "after" revealed. Native fit for
   Qolca (manual process → automated).
5. **Mistakes to avoid** — negativity bias; outperforms the positive framing of
   the same content.
6. **Data deep-dive** — one number per slide. Needs real citable stats (we have
   them for ComeHomeTag).
7. **Case study / teardown** — the B2B workhorse for LinkedIn.
8. **Template / swipe file** — literally built to be saved. Propaga's home turf.

## 4. Design system

### Canvases

| Platform | Canvas | Slides | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | **1080×1350** (4:5) | 8–10 | max screen real estate in feed |
| TikTok photo | **1080×1920** (9:16) | 5–10 | needs a sound attached |
| Facebook | 1080×1350 | 8–10 | cross-post; not a primary channel |
| LinkedIn | **1080×1350 PDF** | 5–15 | PDF only — PPTX/DOCX render inconsistently |

### Safe zones — the thing that silently ruins carousels

- **IG feed**: bottom ~150–200px is under the like/save UI and caption preview.
  Top ~120px can be crowded by the handle on small devices.
- **IG profile grid**: a 4:5 post is cropped to a **centered 1080×1080 square** —
  anything in the top or bottom **270px** is invisible on your own profile grid.
  Logos parked in the bottom corner disappear from the grid view.
- **TikTok**: top ~100px (tabs), bottom ~300px (caption/username/sound), right
  ~150px (like/comment/share rail).

> **Design rule that solves all of it at once:** lay out every slide inside a
> **1080×1350 core**, and keep all text inside a **centered 1080×1080 box** with
> ~90px padding. Then render 9:16 for TikTok by *extending the background* above
> and below. Because our backgrounds are abstract (§5), extending them is free —
> no re-layout, no second design pass. **One design → every platform.**

### Typography **[CRAFT]**

- Minimum 24–28px equivalent for body; headline is the largest thing on the slide
  by a wide margin. On a 1080-wide canvas that means headlines around **72–110px**
  and body **44–56px** — much bigger than feels right in a desktop preview.
- Contrast: **4.5:1** for body, **3:1** for large text (WCAG AA). Over a photo or
  texture, that means a scrim/overlay, not hope.
- **Consistency across slides reduces cognitive load** — same type scale, same
  palette, same margins, so the reader spends attention on the message rather
  than re-orienting on every swipe. This is the research-backed justification for
  the reusable-template approach.
- **≤ 20 words per slide.** One idea per slide, always.
- **[MEASURED]** IG's algorithm reads text in images and indexes it — so on-image
  text is also SEO. Keep it legible and keyword-bearing.

## 5. The repeated-background approach — validated, with one caveat

Carlos's instinct (generate a small set of backgrounds once, reuse them forever,
vary only the text) is **correct**, and there's a real risk it also dodges:

**[MEASURED]** AI-slop backlash: engagement drops ~12% when audiences *identify*
content as AI-generated; some studies report 20–35%. Emotional messaging
perceived as AI-written triggers active distrust, which is precisely the failure
mode for ComeHomeTag's fear→relief register.

The nuance that makes this workable:

> **The slop penalty attaches to AI-generated *people, faces, and fake
> scenes* — not to abstract texture.** Nobody has ever felt betrayed by a
> gradient. A generated photo of a smiling family "reuniting" is a liability;
> a generated paper grain, dusk gradient, or bokeh field is just design.

So the rule is:

- **Backgrounds = abstract / textural / non-representational.** Generate once,
  reuse forever, zero marginal cost, zero slop risk.
- **Anything with a human in it = real.** Real photos of Carlos, real product
  shots of the ComeHomeTag tag, real screenshots of Qolca's automations, real
  Propaga UI. These are also the highest-credibility assets we have.
- **[MEASURED]** 91% of consumers expect disclosure when AI is used — another
  reason to keep AI out of anything that looks like documentary evidence.

### The background library

Build **15–25 plates per brand**, at **1080×1920** (so they crop down to 4:5 and
extend for 9:16). Each plate gets a role: `cover`, `value`, `emphasis`, `cta`.
Rotate them; nobody notices a background repeating across weeks — they notice a
brand that looks like a brand. Limit the layout system to **4–5 slide types**
total to prevent brand drift.

### GPT image prompts — background plates

General skeleton (everything after the style clause is what keeps it usable):

```
[STYLE + SUBJECT], [PALETTE], soft even lighting, no text, no letters, no logos,
no people, no recognizable objects, extremely simple composition, large empty
areas, generous negative space in the CENTER of the frame for text overlay,
subtle fine-grain film texture, vertical 9:16 composition, edges that continue
naturally beyond the frame
```

The three clauses that matter most, and why:
- `no text, no letters` — image models hallucinate garbage type constantly, and
  our text is added later at real vector quality.
- `negative space in the CENTER` — most prompt guides say "on the right", which
  is a web-banner habit. Our text is centered in a 1:1 core.
- `edges that continue naturally beyond the frame` — makes 9:16 extension clean.

**ComeHomeTag** (warm, hopeful, protective — never sinister; fear lives in the
words, never in the image):
```
Soft abstract gradient background, warm amber and cream fading into deep teal,
gentle out-of-focus bokeh light in the upper third like late afternoon sun
through a window, no text, no letters, no logos, no people, no objects,
extremely simple, generous negative space in the CENTER for text overlay,
subtle fine-grain film texture, vertical 9:16, edges continuing beyond frame
```
Variants: soft cream paper texture with a faint warm vignette · muted sage-and-
sand gradient · dusk blue-to-apricot wash · pale linen weave, very low contrast.

**Qolca** (technical, precise, confident — B2B credibility, not sci-fi):
```
Minimal abstract technical background, near-black charcoal with a faint
blueprint grid at 6% opacity, one thin electric-cyan diagonal line as the only
accent, no text, no letters, no logos, no people, no icons, no circuit-board
cliches, extremely simple, generous negative space in the CENTER for text
overlay, subtle noise texture, vertical 9:16, edges continuing beyond frame
```
Variants: dark slate with a soft single spotlight gradient · off-white Swiss-grid
paper with one thin accent rule · deep navy with a very faint isometric grid.

**Propaga** (bright, energetic, friendly SaaS — LatAm SMB, not enterprise):
```
Clean bright abstract background, soft coral and warm yellow gradient on
off-white, one large soft-edged geometric shape in a corner, flat modern
illustration style, no text, no letters, no logos, no people, no UI elements,
extremely simple, generous negative space in the CENTER for text overlay,
very subtle paper grain, vertical 9:16, edges continuing beyond frame
```
Variants: mint-to-white wash · lilac and cream with a soft circle · plain
off-white with a thin colored border rule (the workhorse for text-heavy slides).

**Rule for generation:** make each plate in a batch of 4 and keep the flattest,
most boring one. A background that's interesting competes with the headline —
its entire job is to be a legible, on-brand surface.

## 6. Caption & distribution layer

**[MEASURED]** IG indexes **captions, hashtags and alt text** as searchable, and
public professional-account posts now surface in Google. Hashtag-led discovery
gave way to keyword-led search in late 2024. This matches the earlier verified
finding (search +114%, trending audio −59%) — so:

- **Caption opens with the keyword phrase in plain Spanish**, the way someone
  would type it: *"qué hacer si se pierde un niño en un centro comercial"*,
  *"automatizar WhatsApp para clínicas"*, *"ideas de contenido para negocios
  pequeños"*.
- **Alt text on every slide** — describe it naturally with the target term. It's
  a direct ranking factor and, unlike hashtag stuffing, it can't be penalized.
- **On-image text is indexed too** — the headline itself is doing SEO work.
- **TikTok photo posts need a sound** — audio is part of the distribution signal
  even on a photo post. Non-negotiable there, irrelevant on IG.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** "Adding music to an IG carousel pushes it into a different
  algorithm" — widely repeated, no primary source. Cheap to do, so do it; don't
  build a plan on it.
- **CTA ask is "guarda"/"comparte con...", not "comenta"** — we're optimizing
  saves and sends, which are the signals that actually rank.

## 7. Per-brand strategy

Effort split inherited from the 15-day plan: **ComeHomeTag 45% · Qolca 35% ·
Propaga 20%** — but carousels are cheap enough that Propaga can carry more volume
than it did on video.

### 7.1 ComeHomeTag — the WhatsApp-forward play

**Channel priority:** Instagram (primary) → TikTok photo mode → Facebook (this
is the one brand where FB genuinely matters — Peruvian parents and adult children
of elderly parents are actually on Facebook, and FB's format gap is negligible).

**Why carousels fit:** the goal isn't views, it's a mother forwarding a checklist
to her family WhatsApp group. That's a *send*, the highest-value IG signal, and
it's a behavior that a checklist triggers and a Reel doesn't.

**The formula that has to hold every time:** fear and efficacy **in the same
post**. Never a scary statistic without the "and here's what you do" inside the
same carousel — that's the difference between a PSA and fearmongering, and it's
already a verified finding for this niche.

**Pillars:**
1. **Protocolos** — *"Los primeros 10 minutos: qué hacer si se pierde tu hijo"*,
   *"Si tu papá con Alzheimer sale solo de casa"*. Checklist format, one action
   per slide. Highest save rate of anything we can make.
2. **Lugares de riesgo** — playa, centro comercial, procesión, feria, transporte.
   Seasonal and searchable.
3. **Datos que nadie conoce** — 51 desapariciones/día en Perú (52% menores,
   Sidpol ene–jul 2025) · 200K+ peruanos con Alzheimer (MINSA 2019) · 6 de cada
   10 personas con demencia se extravían (alz.org). One stat per carousel, not
   one per slide.
4. **Mitos** — *"El GPS es la única forma"* → sin batería, sin app, sin
   mensualidad. This is where the AngelSense wedge ($45–65/mo) gets used without
   naming a competitor.
5. **Reuniones reales / testimonios** — testimonials beat brand feeds by a wide
   margin in this niche. Real photos only.

**Design:** warm palette, high-contrast headline over a soft plate, real product
photo of the tag on slides 8–9 only. Never a stock-scary image of a lost child;
the emotional weight goes in the copy.

**Cover hooks (ES):**
- *"51 niños desaparecen cada día en Perú."* (stat + loss)
- *"No es un GPS. No tiene batería. No cuesta mensualidad."* (contradiction)
- *"Tu hijo sabe tu nombre. ¿Sabe tu número?"* (question that lands)
- *"Lo que NO debes hacer en los primeros 10 minutos."* (mistakes + loss)

**Slide 2 must stand alone** — for a PSA this is the biggest free win available,
because slide 2 can carry the stat while slide 1 carries the question.

**CTA:** *"Guarda esto. Ojalá nunca lo necesites."* — save-optimized, emotionally
correct, and not a sales ask.

### 7.2 Qolca — LinkedIn carousels are the highest-leverage asset we have

**Channel priority: LinkedIn (Carlos's personal profile) → Instagram → nothing
else.** Two verified reasons: LinkedIn carousels are the highest-engagement
format measured on any platform (21.77%), and personal profiles beat company
pages by ~63%. Also verified: there is **no evidence organic IG/TikTok generates
B2B leads at zero followers** — so IG here is a credibility surface for people
who already met Carlos, not a lead source. Don't over-invest in it.

**Register:** builder, not consultant. *"We build, not just advise"* means the
carousels should contain **real screenshots, real numbers, real timelines**. This
is also the strongest anti-slop position of the three brands — screenshots are
unfakeable-looking in a way AI imagery isn't.

**Pillars:**
1. **Before/after de un proceso** — the manual workflow on the left slides, the
   automated one after. End with hours/month saved. Native carousel archetype.
2. **Teardowns** — *"Cómo construí un agente de WhatsApp para una clínica en 6
   días"*, one build step per slide, screenshots throughout.
3. **Por industria** — e-commerce, inmobiliaria, clínicas, logística. *"5 procesos
   que tu [industria] sigue haciendo a mano en 2026."* Each one is a
   keyword-targeted lead magnet and they're near-identical to produce.
4. **Costos reales** — what an automation actually costs to build and run. Nobody
   in the Lima market publishes this; it's the fastest trust-builder available.
5. **Errores** — *"4 razones por las que tu chatbot no vende nada."*

**Format:** 1080×1350 PDF, 8–12 pages, dark technical plates, one idea per page,
a real screenshot every 3rd page. The same content re-renders to IG 4:5 with no
changes.

**CTA:** a specific, low-friction ask — *"¿Qué proceso te está comiendo más
horas? Escríbeme"* — not "book a call."

### 7.3 Propaga — the product is the content

**Channel priority:** Instagram → TikTok photo mode.

**The structural advantage:** Propaga is a social-media tool, its audience is
LatAm SMBs and community managers, and carousels are exactly what that audience
saves. A carousel about how to make good carousels *is* the demo. Every post is
simultaneously education, proof of competence, and a product argument.

**Pillars:**
1. **Swipe files / templates** — *"30 ideas de contenido para tu negocio este
   mes"*, *"15 hooks que puedes copiar hoy"*. Built to be saved; the highest
   save-rate content that exists.
2. **Calendarios** — a month planned out, one week per slide. Seasonal LatAm
   dates (Fiestas Patrias, Día de la Madre, Navidad) — searchable and recurring.
3. **Noticias de plataforma en español** — algorithm changes explained in plain
   Spanish, fast. English-language social news is 2–3 weeks ahead of the Spanish
   coverage; that gap is an unoccupied niche and it's free content.
4. **Errores de CM** — *"Por qué tus posts no llegan a nadie (y no es el
   algoritmo)"*.
5. **Antes/después de un perfil** — a real account cleanup, bio → grid → captions.

**Design:** the brightest, most template-driven system of the three — this brand
should look like a tool. Text-heavy slides on the plain off-white plate; the
colored plates for covers and CTAs only.

**CTA:** *"Guarda esto para tu próxima semana de contenido"* → profile → free
tier. The $10/mo price point converts on utility, and the free tier means the ask
is tiny.

## 8. Production system (sketch — build after this is approved)

The strategy above implies a specific tool, and it's small:

```
brands/<brand>/
  brand.json          palette, fonts, type scale, margins, logo
  plates/*.png        the 15–25 reusable backgrounds (1080×1920)
  posts/<slug>.json   { hook, slides:[{role, headline, body}], caption, alt[] }
  out/<slug>/         01.png … 08.png  (4:5)  +  /tiktok/ (9:16)
```

A post is **just text** — a JSON of 8 short strings. The renderer picks plates by
role, lays out type on the 1080×1080 core with auto-fit, and exports both
aspects plus the LinkedIn PDF. Writing a carousel becomes writing 8 lines,
which is the only part that should ever cost time.

Reuses what CLIPPING already has: the FastAPI + Vite localhost pattern,
`paths.py`-style filesystem-as-database, atomic JSON writes. It needs no ffmpeg
and no ML — the rendering is HTML/CSS → PNG (headless Chromium) or Pillow.
Deliberately **separate from the clip factory**: no shared state, its own folder,
its own port.

Open questions for Carlos before building:
1. Fonts per brand — do Propaga and Qolca have defined brand fonts, or do we pick?
2. Is the LinkedIn PDF export needed in v1, or is Qolca posting manually at first?
3. Does this want a UI at all in v1, or is "edit JSON → hit render" enough?

## 9. What to do first (concrete)

1. **Generate the 3 plate libraries** with the §5 prompts (≈20 plates each,
   ~60 images total, one sitting).
2. **Lock one 8-slide template** — cover / re-hook / 5 value / CTA — and prove it
   on ComeHomeTag's *"Los primeros 10 minutos"* checklist, by hand, once.
3. **Post it and read the two numbers that matter**: slide-1→2 swipe-through
   (target 60–75%) and save rate (target 1.5–3%). Both are in IG Insights.
4. Only then build the renderer — the template has to be validated by a real post
   before it's worth automating.
